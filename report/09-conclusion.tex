\section{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

This work presented a custom-built, open-source test framework for automated power consumption measurement of consumer network devices.
Using a DECT~200 smart plug for power metering and a Go-based load generator with a web-based analysis dashboard, we conducted five test campaigns across four consumer-grade devices (Fritzbox~7530, Huawei~EG8245W5-8T, Asus~RT-AX68U, and Alcatel~HH40V), addressing eight research questions.

Key findings:
\begin{description}
    \item[RQ1 (Port Count vs.\ Power):]
    All devices show measurable power increases under load (6--16\% above idle), but the per-port increment under full traffic is small (30--175\,mW).
    CPU-limited devices (Fritzbox) show non-monotonic power profiles due to CPU saturation.

    \item[RQ2 (Per-Port PHY Cost):]
    Connecting an idle GbE cable costs 265--302\,mW on average; Fast Ethernet costs $\sim$172\,mW.
    The per-port idle overhead dominates over the marginal cost of actual data transmission (Test~2 showed only 4.9--7.1\% power increase from idle to full line rate).

    \item[RQ3 (Power vs.\ Throughput):]
    Power scales sub-linearly with throughput for all devices.
    The base platform power dominates, and reducing idle power (through device selection or power-saving modes) has far greater impact than limiting throughput.

    \item[RQ4 (Idle Power):]
    Bare-minimum power varies from 1.14\,W (Alcatel) to 6.34\,W (Huawei).
    With all subsystems idle, power ranges from 2.30\,W (Alcatel) to 7.99\,W (Asus), translating to annual costs of \euro{5}--\euro{18} at \euro{0.25}/kWh.

    \item[RQ5 (WiFi Radio Cost):]
    Idle WiFi radios are the dominant subsystem power consumer, adding 0.4--2.7\,W depending on device and number of bands.
    WiFi power per unit throughput is 7--8$\times$ higher than wired Ethernet.
    The Asus achieves the best WiFi energy efficiency (66\,Mbps/W at 5\,GHz near) while the Huawei is the least efficient (3.7\,Mbps/W at 2.4\,GHz near).
    Distance effects are device- and band-dependent, not universal.

    \item[RQ6 (Power-Saving Modes):]
    The Fritzbox eco mode is the most effective, saving 474--670\,mW (7--19\%) across all subsystems, at the cost of Ethernet throughput reduction to $\sim$95\,Mbps per port.
    Asus and Huawei TX power reduction yields minimal or inconsistent savings.

    \item[RQ7 (Rated vs.\ Real Maximum):]
    Even with all subsystems at maximum load, devices reach only 22--47\% of their rated power.
    Manufacturer specifications include substantial headroom, likely accounting for worst-case scenarios (e.g., DSL modem active, maximum client count, sustained retransmissions).

    \item[RQ8 (Bridge Mode):]
    Splitting an ISP modem into bridge mode plus a dedicated router increases total system idle power by 85.6\% ($\sim$16.6\,W combined vs.\ $\sim$8.96\,W standalone).
    Bridge mode is not energetically favorable for power reduction.
\end{description}

\subsection{Limitations}

The results are subject to several limitations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The DECT~200 smart plug has a resolution of $\sim$70\,mW and a native update interval of $\sim$2\,min (improved to $\sim$15\,s via Home Assistant polling hack), limiting precision for fine-grained per-port analysis.
    \item All Ethernet traffic targeted the device's CPU (UDP to device IP) rather than exercising the hardware switching fabric. Real-world forwarding workloads may show different power profiles.
    \item WiFi throughput in Test~3 was measured manually via the iperf3 app on smartphones, introducing human observation error. Test~4 provides more precise automated WiFi measurements.
    \item The Fritzbox developed an intermittent port anomaly during Test~3 that required throughput interpolation from working ports. Power measurements were unaffected.
    \item Tests were conducted with a single sample of each device. Manufacturing variation and firmware differences may affect reproducibility.
    \item No zero-cable baseline was measured in Test~1 (cables were already connected during the pre-test phase), which is why Test~3 was needed to isolate the true bare-minimum power floor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Work}

Possible extensions of this work include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Long-term (24h+) idle power measurements to capture potential sleep-state transitions and diurnal power patterns.
    \item Layer~2 switching load tests to measure power under realistic forwarding workloads rather than CPU-targeted traffic.
    \item Testing additional device categories: managed switches, mesh WiFi systems, and enterprise access points.
    \item Investigating the relationship between CPU temperature and power consumption (accessible via web interfaces on some devices).
    \item Measuring the power impact of firmware updates and security features (firewall rules, VPN, QoS).
\end{itemize}

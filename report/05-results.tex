\paragraph{RQ1 --- Port count vs.\ power.}
Per-port power increments range from 30--175\,mW, small relative to idle power.
The Fritzbox shows a non-monotonic profile: power at 3 ports (5040\,mW) is \emph{lower} than at 2 ports (5055\,mW) because CPU saturation reduces throughput and thus total work done.
The Huawei and Asus scale monotonically---their CPUs are not the bottleneck at 4$\times$1\,Gbps.

\paragraph{RQ4 (partial) --- Idle power.}
Idle power ranges from 1.85\,W (Alcatel) to 8.96\,W (Huawei), a 4.8$\times$ difference.
Annual idle costs range from \euro{4}--\euro{20} at \euro{0.25}/kWh.
Note: these are ``cables connected'' measurements, not bare-minimum standby (covered in Test~3).

\paragraph{RQ7 (partial) --- Rated vs.\ real maximum.}
Under Ethernet-only load, devices reach only 21--40\% of their rated maximum.
The remaining headroom is accounted for by WiFi, USB, and other subsystems not exercised in this test (see Test~3 for full-subsystem results).

\paragraph{Measurement notes.}
The DECT~200 resolution ($\sim$70\,mW steps) limits per-port precision.
All traffic targeted the device's CPU (UDP to its own IP), not the switching ASIC.
Transient throughput spikes in some data points are measurement artifacts from the 5-second polling window.

\subsection{Summary: RQ2 and RQ3}
\label{sec:test2-rq-answers}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{RQ2:} Each idle GbE connection adds 120--395\,mW. Disconnecting unused cables offers a measurable, though modest, saving---especially for the Asus and Huawei where idle cable overhead exceeds per-port load cost.

\paragraph{RQ3:} Power scales sub-linearly with throughput. The total idle-to-full-load increase is only 4.9--7.1\% of idle power, meaning the base platform dominates. Reducing idle power (device selection, eco modes) matters far more than limiting throughput.

Power measurements are unaffected.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Distance and Band Analysis}
\label{sec:results-wifi-distance-band}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Distance effect on power.}
Moving the client behind a wall does \emph{not} consistently increase router power.
The Asus at 5\,GHz is an exception: overhead \emph{increases} from 2\,992 to 3\,751\,mW at the wall (+25\%), suggesting active TX power boosting despite 58\% throughput loss (584$\to$244\,Mbps).
At 2.4\,GHz the opposite happens: overhead drops 68\% (3\,101$\to$996\,mW) because the radio is mostly idle at 33\,Mbps.
The Fritzbox and Huawei show modest, band-dependent changes.
The Alcatel draws 47\% more at the wall position with barely any throughput reduction (94$\to$88\,Mbps), likely due to retransmission overhead.

\paragraph{Band effect.}
5\,GHz achieves 2--4$\times$ higher throughput at near distance (Asus: 584 vs.\ 143\,Mbps; Fritzbox: 235 vs.\ 89\,Mbps) thanks to wider channel bandwidths.
However, 5\,GHz degrades more through walls---the Fritzbox drops 74\% on 5\,GHz vs.\ 43\% on 2.4\,GHz, confirming that lower frequencies penetrate walls better.

\paragraph{Huawei variability.}
The Huawei showed extremely erratic throughput (0--253\,Mbps swings, 743--836 TCP retransmits) because its WiFi-to-WiFi topology means both TX and RX traverse the wireless medium, doubling congestion impact.

\paragraph{Alcatel ``both WiFi'' tests.}
To check whether the 100\,Mbps LAN bottlenecked WiFi throughput, additional runs placed the test PC on WiFi too.
Throughput actually \emph{decreased} (86\,Mbps near, 64\,Mbps wall), confirming the LAN was not the bottleneck---the 802.11n radio caps at $\sim$94\,Mbps.

\subsubsection{Analysis (RQ8)}

The two-device setup draws +7\,670\,mW (+85.6\%) more at idle, almost entirely from the Asus's idle power (8\,226\,mW).
Subtracting the Asus's Test~1 values, the Huawei in bridge mode draws $\sim$8\,406\,mW idle ($-$556\,mW vs.\ router mode) and $\sim$8\,139\,mW at full load ($-$1\,371\,mW)---bridge mode does save 6--14\% on the ISP device by disabling NAT/DHCP/firewall.
However, the Asus's 8.2\,W idle draw dwarfs this saving.

\textbf{RQ8 answer: No.} Bridge mode is not energetically favorable.
The $\sim$8\,W penalty ($\sim$70\,kWh/year, $\sim$\euro{25}) should only be accepted when the dedicated router provides features the ISP device cannot.

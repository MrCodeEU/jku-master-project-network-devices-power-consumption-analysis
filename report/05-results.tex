\paragraph{RQ1: Port count vs.\ power.}
Per-port power increases are surprisingly small at just 30--175\,mW, relative to idle power. Adding load on ports costs much less than the idle cost of having the cables plugged in (see Section \ref{sec:test2-port-overhead}).
The Fritzbox shows a non-monotonic profile: power at 3 ports (5040\,mW) is \emph{lower} than at 2 ports (5055\,mW) because \ac{CPU} saturation means less actual work is done.
The Huawei and Asus scale mostly monotonically as their CPUs are not the bottleneck at 4$\times$1\,Gbps.

\paragraph{RQ4 (partial): Idle power.}
Idle power ranges from 1.85\,W (Alcatel) to 8.96\,W (Huawei) -- nearly a 5$\times$ spread between the cheapest and most power-hungry device.
Annual idle costs are \euro{4}--\euro{20} at \euro{0.25}/kWh~\cite{bib:eurostat-electricity}.
Note: these measurements have cables connected; bare-minimum power (no cables, Wi-Fi off) is covered in Test~3.

\paragraph{RQ7 (partial): Rated vs.\ real maximum.}
With only Ethernet active, devices reach 21--40\% of their rated maximum power. The spec sheet numbers have a lot of headroom.
Adding Wi-Fi, \ac{USB}, and other subsystems closes the gap further but never fully. See Test~3 for the full picture.

\paragraph{Key observations.}
The Asus shows: the Wi-Fi radios alone cost 2\,656\,mW more than all four Ethernet ports put together. Active Wi-Fi load on top adds another 3\,346\,mW. That is a lot of power just for wireless. Wich makes sense given the broader channel bandwidth.
The Huawei starts high (6\,342\,mW bare minimum), likely because the GPON fiber transceiver is always running, there is no way to turn it off. Under full load it reaches 47.1\% of it's rated 24\,W which is the closest any device gets to its rated maximum power draw.
The Fritzbox can't enable Wi-Fi bands one at a time. Therefore, both radios turn on together (+1\,745\,mW in one step). Under full load it hits 37.7\% of rated 18\,W.
The Alcatel is remarkable for how little it draws: below 2.3\,W even at full load. Its Wi-Fi load phase actually \emph{reduced} power by 95\,mW. It appears to not scale power consumption with throughput at all.

\subsubsection{Analysis (RQ8)}

The two-device setup draws +7\,670\,mW (+85.6\%) more at idle than the Huawei alone.
Almost all of that is just the Asus existing: it draws 8\,226\,mW at idle regardless of what the Huawei is doing.
The Huawei in bridge mode does save something compared to the data from Test~1. It draws $\sim$556\,mW less at idle and $\sim$1\,371\,mW less at full load (6--14\% reduction).
But the Asus's 8.2\,W idle is much higher than the savings.

\textbf{RQ8 answer:} No. Bridge mode is not an energy-saving strategy.
The two-device setup costs about $\sim$70\,kWh extra per year. The only reason to do it is if you genuinely need features the \ac{ISP} modem doesn't provide or security features or performance features like better WiFi coverage.
